Mountain Front Sinuosity (\(S_{mf}\))

Module: rockmorph.tools.smf.SMFEngine

Description

Mountain Front Sinuosity (\(S_{mf}\)) is a quantitative morphometric index used in tectonic geomorphology to evaluate the balance between active tectonic uplift and erosional slope degradation along mountain range fronts (Bull & McFadden, 1977; Keller & Pinter, 2002).

Tectonically active fronts associated with normal or thrust faulting are regularly refreshed by displacement, yielding straight, low-sinuosity scarps. Conversely, when tectonic uplift slows or ceases, surface runoff, stream incision, and mass wasting processes progressively dissect the scarp, pushing the front landward and generating a sinuous, irregular front.

Mathematical Formulation

The index is defined as the ratio of the sinuous length of the mountain front to its straight-line baseline:

\[S_{mf} = \frac{L_f}{L_r}\]

Where:

  • \(L_f\): The sinuous length of the mountain-plain transition boundary (measured geodesically on the WGS84 ellipsoid).
  • \(L_r\): The straight-line length of the baseline (representing the underlying fault strike or structural trend).

Baseline Estimation Options (\(L_r\))

Standard geomorphological workflows rely on a simple endpoint-to-endpoint distance. However, for highly sinuous or curved fronts, this approach may underestimate the true baseline length. RockMorph supports two modes:

  1. Direct Endpoints: Strictly measures the geodesic distance between the first and last vertices of the front.
  2. PCA Axis (Structural Trend): Computes the Principal Component Analysis (covariance matrix) of all vertices' coordinates. The first principal component (PC1) represents the dominant structural strike, and \(L_r\) is determined by projecting the extreme vertices along this axis.

Tectonic Activity Classification

Based on empirical works (Rockwell et al., 1985; Silva et al., 2003), range fronts are classified into three levels of relative tectonic activity:

Index Range Tectonic Class Geomorphic Interpretation
\(S_{mf} < 1.4\) Class 1 (Active) Highly active structures. High uplift rates, minimal erosional embayment.
\(1.4 \le S_{mf} < 1.8\) Class 2 (Moderate) Moderately active. Transition phase with concurrent uplift and incision.
\(S_{mf} \ge 1.8\) Class 3 (Inactive) Inactive, stable, or highly eroded fronts. Erosional processes dominate.

Methodology & MFSD Algorithm

RockMorph allows manual tracing of range fronts, but also features the Mountain-Flank Skeleton Detector (MFSD). This automated detector ensures reproducible, objective results across large regional DEM coverages, preventing human digitization bias.

Algorithmic Pipeline:

  1. Nodata Repair: Internal gaps and edges are filled using nearest-valid Euclidean distance transform mapping. This guarantees that the scarp detection algorithm cannot track the artifact borders of the parent mosaic.
  2. Relief and Slope Extraction: Slope percentage and regional relief (maximum elevation minus minimum elevation in a moving spatial window, e.g. 1500m) are calculated. A pixel is initially classified as a mountain flank if both values exceed the custom thresholds: \[\text{Flank Pixel} \iff (\text{Slope} \ge \text{Threshold}_{\text{slope}}) \land (\text{Relief} \ge \text{Threshold}_{\text{relief}})\]
  3. Erosion Buffer: To completely eliminate raster-border tracking, a safety buffer (erosion step) is applied near outer nodata pixels.
  4. Morphological Cleaning: Mathematical morphology operators (closing using disk-shaped structuring elements sized in real ground meters, and opening) close valley gaps and eliminate isolated pixel noise.
  5. Skeletal Tracing: The clean binary massif mask is reduced to 1-pixel-thick centerlines using skeletonization. Connected component graphs are constructed. The algorithm identifies closed loops (representing isolated massifs) and traces their perimeters, while open chains (linear fronts) are traced using a double-BFS (Breadth-First Search) graph diameter to extract the longest simple path.
  6. Fragment Collinear Merging: Nearby segment endpoints are checked for proximity and collinearity. If their gap distance is within \( \text{Max Gap} \) and their angle is less than \( \text{Max Angle} \), they are merged into unified geological range fronts.

Tectonic Classification & Output Symbology

When computation completes, RockMorph automatically styles the output vector range fronts using a professional categorized renderer on the QGIS Map Canvas. The fronts are colored and styled strictly based on their calculated \(S_{mf}\) value to reflect relative tectonic activity:

Tectonic Class Default Color Quantitative Threshold Geomorphic Field Interpretation
Class 1 (Active) Red
(#e74c3c)		 \(S_{mf} < 1.4\) Highly active range fronts. Associated with high slip rates, rapid tectonic uplift, and undeveloped alluvial fans. The front remains straight, pristine, and aligned with the active fault line.
Class 2 (Moderate) Orange
(#e67e22)		 \(1.4 \le S_{mf} < 1.8\) Moderately active. Represents a transitional phase where tectonic uplift is balanced or slightly outpaced by stream erosion, resulting in initial structural embayments.
Class 3 (Inactive) Green
(#2ec86b)		 \(S_{mf} \ge 1.8\) Inactive or stable ranges. Tectonic activity has ceased. Surface processes (erosion, mass wasting) dominate, creating deep structural valleys and highly sinuous front geometries.

Parameters Reference & Field Guide

Choosing the correct thresholds is critical to obtaining geomorphologically meaningful range fronts. Below is an advanced guide on how to tune each parameter depending on your regional tectonic context and DEM resolution.

Parameter Type [Default] Geomorphological Implication Tuning & Regional Context
dem_layer QgsRasterLayer
[Mandatory]		 The primary Digital Elevation Model. Controls the spatial resolution of slope and relief derivative matrices. Resolution advice: 10m to 30m resolution (e.g., Cop-30, SRTM) is optimal for regional fronts. Avoid sub-metric drone DEMs unless aggressively downsampled, as micro-topography (soil creep, vegetation) acts as high-frequency noise.
scarp_layer QgsVectorLayer
[None]		 Optional line vector layer representing pre-mapped ranges. If provided, the automated detection engine is bypassed. Perfect for comparing automated results with structural range fronts mapped by hand in the field or digitized from classic structural geology papers.
unit_field String
[None]		 Optional attribute column in the scarp layer specifying geomorphological or structural range units. Used to group outputs by tectonic structures in tables and exports. If provided, the QGIS layer legend will allow you to quickly filter and analyze specific fault segments individually.
baseline_method String
["endpoints"]		 Determines the straight-line baseline (\(L_r\)) calculation method used as the denominator for \(S_{mf}\). Use "endpoints" for simple, straight normal-fault scarps. Switch to "pca" for complex, highly curved, folded thrust systems where range-front orientations change along strike; PCA calculates the dominant structural trend more objectively.
min_slope_pct Float
[15.0 %]		 Sets the lower slope gradient limit to classify range-front cells. Filters out flat sedimentary plains from massifs. High value (> 25%): Restricts tracing to steep, young, highly active scarps. Breaks continuity in older, degraded ranges.
Low value (< 10%): Captures degraded foothills or gentle slopes, but increases noise from alluvial fans or old landslide deposits.
min_relief_m Float
[100.0 m]		 Establishes the minimum regional height difference (maximum - minimum elevation) required to validate a massif structure. High-relief regions (Alps, Himalayas): Increase to 250m–500m to isolate major mountain ranges and ignore moderate internal ridges.
Low-relief rifts (Intracontinental): Decrease to 50m to capture subtle tectonic fault scarps without triggering false-positives on flat terrains.
relief_window_m Float
[1500.0 m]		 Specifies the diameter of the moving window used to analyze elevation differences. This must match the expected width of your range's foot slope transition. Too large a window (e.g., > 3000m) smooths out and generalizes the local step. Too small a window (e.g., < 500m) fails to capture macro-structural relief and gets stuck in local gullies.
max_gap_m Float
[1500.0 m]		 The maximum spatial gap (in ground meters) the algorithm is allowed to bridge to merge adjacent scarp fragments. Highly dissected range fronts are frequently cut by wide transverse river valleys (gorges). Increase this value to reconnect segments across valleys. However, setting this too high may falsely bridge independent range structures across sedimentary basins.
max_merge_angle_deg Float
[45.0 °]		 The maximum angular strike deviation allowed between two neighboring scarp fragments to merge them. Strike-slip or planar faults: Set to low values (15°–25°) to restrict merging to straight, continuous scarps.
Fold-and-thrust belts: Increase to 45°–60° to allow the algorithm to trace highly sinuous, curved, or scalloped fronts.
